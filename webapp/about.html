About

Giving hands a voice.

Millions of people speak with their hands, yet most of the world can't read a single sign. Hastavāṇī is a small step across that gap: point a webcam at your hands and it turns American and Indian Sign Language into text and speech — letters, numbers and everyday sentences — right in your browser.

What it can do

🔤 Read fingerspelling

All 26 letters in ASL and ISL, locked in as you sign, built into words and spoken aloud.

💬 Recognise sentences

101 everyday sentences in each language, with reference clips — and it adapts to your own signing.

➗ Count and calculate

Sign digits 0–9 into a calculator or a 10-question practice game with hints and streaks.

📖 Teach the basics

A photo guide to both alphabets, plus digit charts for each language.

Hastavāṇī is a learning and practice companion. It is not a replacement for a human interpreter: real sign language carries grammar, facial expression and nuance far beyond what a webcam model can read today.

Honest limits

We'd rather tell you where it struggles than have you find out the hard way.

  • ASL J and Z are drawn in the air, but the letter reader looks at one pose at a time, so those two are approximations.
  • The ISL alphabet follows the public dataset it learned from, which differs from the official ISLRTC chart for a few letters, and comes from a single signer — accuracy on other people is still unmeasured.
  • Sentences come from a fixed catalog, not open translation. For someone it has never seen, ISL picks the exact sentence about 20% of the time (32% within its top three) — so teach it your signing for the sentences you use. ASL sentences are built from single-word clips and are an earlier experiment still.
  • Digits 7 and 8 couldn't be checked against real photos yet; the other digits were right 97% of the time in testing.
  • Good light, a plain background and your whole upper body in frame make a big difference. You'll need a camera and a modern browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari).

FAQ

Why does it not detect my hand?+

Keep the whole hand in frame with even lighting, away from a busy or very dark background. ISL letters and most sentences need both hands visible.

A letter keeps flipping between two answers.+

Some letters look alike (ASL M, N and S, for example). Hold the sign steady for a second with your palm toward the camera.

How do I get better sentence results?+

Sit back so your head, shoulders and both hands are in view, watch the reference clip first, and sign at a natural pace. Then teach it: after each result press Correct — remember it, or pick the right sentence and Save as this. Two or three saved takes of a sentence help a lot.

Where is my history kept, and how do I delete it?+

Only in this browser, under your account. Open History from your profile menu to search, filter, delete single entries or clear everything.

Does it work on my phone?+

Yes, in a modern mobile browser, as long as the site is served over HTTPS so the camera is allowed. Prop the phone up so both hands are free.

Can I turn the voice off?+

Yes — the translator, sentence builder and math page each have a voice toggle. You can still have a finished word spoken with one tap.

Which ISL chart does it follow?+

The one used by the public dataset the letter model was trained on. The alphabet guide shows every sign it expects.
